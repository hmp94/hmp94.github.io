Research Assistant
Research Intern — Semantic Memory Lab 2026 — present
Donders Institute for Brain, Cognition and Behaviour · Radboud University, Nijmegen
Working with Dr. Sachuriga on rodent spatial navigation using the Hex Maze paradigm. Investigating how functional hippocampal cells support schema learning, and testing whether left–right alternating theta sweeps reproduce in our CA1 Neuropixels recordings.
Hex Maze Theta Sweeps Neuropixels Bayesian Decoding Spatial Navigation
Details

During running, position decoded from entorhinal and hippocampal populations does not sit on the animal: within each theta cycle it runs ahead along the direction of travel and returns — a theta sweep. Recording medial entorhinal cortex and parasubiculum with Neuropixels probes, Vollan, Gardner, Moser & Moser (Nature, 2025) reported that consecutive sweeps alternate side: left of the path, then right, then left. The side flipped in 79.8 % of adjacent-cycle triplets against 61.1 % in shuffled data, and roughly half of running theta cycles carried a sweep. Their hypothesis is functional — that the alternation lets the map cover both sides of the path ahead within successive cycles.

Why ask it of this dataset

Our recordings are CA1, and the animals run the 9 × 5 m HexMaze: 40 cm corridor segments meeting at 49 choice points, with a fixed goal and varying start positions, so the layout has to be learned as a schema.

The maze, drawn from the session's own node list with the hexagons regularised onto an ideal lattice. Each segment is 40 cm and filled dots mark the 49 choice points. The long corridors joining the four clusters are absent from the node file, so they are recovered from the animal's tracking and drawn dotted.

Alternating sweeps would matter in this environment. At every choice point the animal has two ways forward, so a map that covers both before the turn is a candidate substrate for schema-guided choice. The reproduction also tests the original result where it is weakest: one synapse downstream of entorhinal cortex, in an environment with far more branch points than a linear track.

What is different here
  • Region and ensemble size — MEC and parasubiculum, 769 cells per session on average and up to 1,522. Here, one CA1 probe: 42–83 well-isolated pyramidal cells per session, roughly sixteen times fewer.
  • Where theta phase comes from — the paper derives phase from a principal component of population spiking. At this population size that fails outright — it recovers about 0.9 Hz, well out of band — so phase is taken from the LFP, which gives 7.9 Hz.
  • Scale of the environment — a 9 × 5 m maze is not a 1.5 m arena. Spatial bins are 5 cm rather than 2.5 cm, smoothing is 7.5 cm, and the decodable region is the visited path dilated by 22.5 cm so that a sweep is able to leave it.
  • Which decoder — the released MATLAB scores positions by population-vector correlation, while the Methods text describes Bayesian reconstruction. Everything here is the Bayesian version, with spike counts smoothed to about 50 ms.
  • Nothing added on top — head direction comes from travel, since the DLC keypoints in these files are unusable. Gates that would reject backward sweeps, or sweeps starting far from the animal, are implemented but left off.
How the pipeline works, step by step
1
Cut the recording into theta cycles

A sweep is defined within a single theta cycle. The LFP is band-passed to 5–10 Hz and Hilbert-transformed for an instantaneous phase, re-zeroed to the theta trough — the point at which the population fires least — and a cycle begins at each upward crossing. Cycles outside 0.08–0.22 s are discarded.

Spikes and tracking are binned at 10 ms, the resolution a sweep requires: one sweep out and back occupies roughly 125 ms.

2
Learn where each cell fires

The decoder needs a template. Using only bins in which the animal ran above 5 cm/s, spike counts and dwell time are accumulated per 5 cm square, smoothed, and divided, giving firing rate per cell per square. Squares occupied for less than 0.25 s are dropped.

3
Decode position from the population

For one 10 ms bin we hold a spike count per cell and want a probability for every position in the maze. Under a Poisson assumption cells contribute independently, so the log-posterior is a sum over cells:

log P(x | n)  =  Σi ni log fi(x)  −  τ Σi fi(x)  +  log P(x)

The first term rewards positions where the cells that fired have high rates. The second penalises positions where silent cells would have been expected to fire, which is what makes silence informative. The prior is flat.

The surface is then reduced to a single point — not its maximum, but a weighted centroid of the top 1 % of the posterior within 10 cm, following the authors' released code. A bin is used only if its peak beats a 99th-percentile spike-shuffle and at least one cell was active.

4
Measure the sweep against an anchor

A sweep is a departure, so it needs a reference. During the first 40 ms of a cycle the decode has not yet departed; decoding those bins and smoothing across neighbouring cycles estimates true position. That is the anchor, and a sweep is decoded minus anchor.

Within each cycle the longest smooth run of decoded points around the peak of population firing is grown, and accepted only if it never jumps more than 20 cm or turns more than 90°, holds at least 4 points, is straight (r² > 0.5), and the animal ran above 15 cm/s. Fold-back tails are trimmed. Prevalence is the fraction of running cycles holding an accepted sweep.

5
Test whether the side alternates

Take every run of three consecutive cycles that each contain a sweep, label each sweep left or right of heading, and count how often the side flips across the triplet.

The null shifts each cell's spikes circularly in time, preserving firing rate and rhythm while destroying the link to place. To count as alternating, the observed value must clear the 99.9th percentile of that shuffle.

What it looks like in our data
Decoded position overlaid on the maze camera (Rat 6, session 5). The white circle is the animal; the cyan-to-magenta trail is position decoded cycle by cycle, labelled by the side it departs to. These are the only 2 of 15 adjacent-cycle triplets in the session that alternate L → R → L, and that count is a loose one — it still includes backward sweeps.
The same two triplets in maze coordinates. Maze in pale grey, the animal's path in dark grey, the rat drawn at its heading; the open circle is the anchor each sweep departs from. Blue sweeps go left, red go right.
Where it stands

Sweep-like events are real structure: prevalence beats the circular spike-shift shuffle in every session, 3.3× on average. The alternation is not. Observed left–right alternation stays below the 99.9th percentile of the shuffle in all ten sessions, and 46 % of sweeps point behind the animal rather than ahead — near the 50 % expected from random directions. Requiring sweeps to run forward removes the mild above-chance alternation that remains.

The likeliest cause: at 10 ms — the timescale a sweep needs — the population carries no decodable position. A cross-validated decoder sits at chance, 253 cm error against a 252 cm null that predicts the training centroid, and 19–52 % of running 10 ms bins contain no spikes from any recorded cell. Heavier smoothing surfaces sweep-like trajectories, but not forward alternating ones. Speed gating, theta phase from spiking, state-space size, cross-validated smoothing width, head direction from DLC, and a genuine LFP timestamp bug in 6 of 10 files were each ruled out with their own decisive number.

Honours Research Intern 2025
University of Amsterdam · Dr. Filip van Opstal
Studied mental resource depletion via pupillometry, testing whether resources depleted by one cognitive domain can interfere with another.
Pupillometry Cognitive Resources Mind Wandering
Details

It has been known that when one gets tired they tend to lose focus and mind-wander. The scientific way to frame this: mental resource depletion causes mind-wandering. However, it is unknown whether depleting mental resources by one module (e.g., spatial processing during a mental rotation task) causes mind-wandering in a different module (e.g., verbal processing).

This study investigates this by first depleting mental resources with one task in one period, then either switching or continuing the task in subsequent periods. We aim to see whether switching vs. continuing the same/different task causes any difference in mind-wandering, operationalized by the frequency and distance of pupil position away from the task-centered location.

Procedure
Period 1: ART (spatial · baseline)
Indicate whether the rotated letter is mirrored or normal
↓ Resources Depletion Phase ↓
Period 2: Switch or Continue
Dot Rotation (spatial · continue)
Indicate whether the relative position of 3 dots changed after rotation
Sternberg (verbal · switch)
Indicate whether the lowercase probe appeared in the memorized sequence
↓
Period 3: ART (spatial · post-depletion)
Same task as Period 1 — measure mind-wandering change
Main DV: Difference in mind-wandering (pupil deviation from task center) between Period 3 and Period 1. If switching modules restores resources, the switch condition should show less mind-wandering increase than the continue condition.

Personal Project
Bachelor's Thesis 2025 — 2026
University of Amsterdam · Dr. Romke Rouw
Investigated cross-dimensional tactile interference between perceived softness and stickiness. Methods included logistic regression and Python-based data analysis and visualization.
Tactile Perception Psychophysics
Details

Whenever we touch a surface, we immediately map its quality onto some subjective tactile description such as how soft it is and how sticky it is. However, this map is not one-to-one, such that we can experience the same tactile feeling with different materials. This study explores under which interaction between physical properties people can perceive the same/different tactile level, in this case of softness and stickiness.

The result of my Bachelor thesis (see Figure 1) reveals that people are more likely to perceive two stimuli as having different perceptual stickiness whether or not they are physically different in stickiness. Interestingly, higher physical softness difference is more likely to trick people this way (Figure 2). Furthermore, the relationship does not reverse, such that physical stickiness does not affect perceptual softness. My personal interpretation of this is thinking of the relative time difference you perceive these two. Softness is perceived when you press onto the surface, and stickiness is when you move away from it. Therefore you can only perceive stickiness after softness, but not the vice versa. Color-Shape Feature Encoding in Visual Short-term Memory 2024
University of Amsterdam · Personal Project
Designed an experiment to study the encoding of color-shape visual features for fragile and working short-term memory using a change detection task.
Visual Short-term Memory Change Detection Feature Encoding
Details

The threshold of what we call "Consciousness" has been a hot topic in cognitive neuroscience. One debate is whether Fragile Visual Short-Term Memory (fVSTM) constitutes a form of conscious perception. Professor Victor Lamme and colleagues have shown fVSTM can encode orientation — a primary visual feature. This experiment extends that paradigm to test whether higher-level features like color and shape are retained in fVSTM versus classic Working Memory (WM).

Stimuli
Blue Square  ·  Green Square  ·  Blue Circle  ·  Green Circle
Procedure — Change Detection Task
fVSTM Trial (fragile · pre-cue)
Cue shown in blank interval before test display — target shape changes
WM Trial (working memory · concurrent)
Cue appears simultaneously with test display — target color changes
Key manipulation: In fVSTM trials the cue appears before the test (during the blank), allowing orientation of attention prior to the test. In WM trials the cue is concurrent with the test display. If fVSTM encodes color/shape, detection accuracy should be above chance in both conditions — testing whether higher-order features reach this pre-attentive memory store.
